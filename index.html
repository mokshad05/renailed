Why it pays

Labour is the biggest lever

At $25/h, Grandview-Woodland's rate is ≈ $560 of de-nailing per saleable tonne C, about the whole $549/t break-even of a Sort & Supply pre-strip C. Halve the minutes per board and recovery starts to pay.

Why it's a lab

It produces the proof judges ask for

Minutes per board, reject rate, 4–6 ft share and slat yield, logged per source house. These replace our T numbers. SFU's Living Lab has funded campus projects before (SFU Living Lab); we treat that as an option, not an assumption.

Later revenue

De-nailing as a service

Once timed, the Lab can quote a per-board fee for small contractors' and makers' lots. Robotic de-nailing reaches 10,000 board feet a day (Green Building Advisor); we stay manual until our own data shows a machine would pay.

Engineer's takeaway

The method is proven in parts

Scan, de-nail, grade, mill is standard practice from hobby shops to robots. Our engineering risk is narrower: whether short bolted slats pass a load test.

Business takeaway

The market already buys the story

Room & Board sold 8,500+ products from Baltimore house wood (JHU News-Letter, 2021). Reclaimed lumber carries a 10–15% premium in Vancouver (Choi, 2025). Our test is whether public-seating buyers pay too.

Honest gap

Where we're different, and unproven

None of these sells installed, re-slattable public benches from 4–6 ft pieces with a per-house tag. That combination is our idea, and the pilot must prove it.

Why plywood belongs in the plan

  • It is the biggest wood stream: 51,748 t of plywood ("composite") is disposed of a year, ≈ 29.7% of the 174,241 t of wood C, and about 5.7× the 9,074 t of clean dimensional lumber C (Tetra Tech, 2026, Table D-1).
  • It is proven elsewhere: in Portland, Sankofa Lumber collects OSB and plywood jobsite scraps, and a millwork shop just south of the city makes them into cross-laminated ¾ in × 4 × 8 ft SecondStory panels for wall cladding, casework, tabletops and furniture, certified FSC Recycled 100% (SnW Wood).
  • It comes from the same lots: a teardown gives old-growth lumber; framing the new house on that lot gives plywood offcuts. Same contractor, two harvests T. (Pre-1950 houses mostly have board sheathing, so most plywood comes from newer buildings and new construction.)
  • Honest differences: panels are glued, so they are an indoor product, separate from our bolted, no-glue outdoor benches. Painted or dirty plywood is rejected, and old adhesives need an emissions check before sale T.

Why mass timber is the long-term headline, not year one

  • Evidence it works: Galway researchers made 3-layer CLT from 1970s roof-truss spruce. Recovered vs new panels: stiffness 10,300 vs 10,900 N/mm², bending strength 49 vs 50 N/mm² (Uí Chúláin et al., 2023).
  • Caveats: only 3 panels per group, one source, Norway spruce (not our Douglas-fir), and only ≈ 28% of the recovered wood ended up in panels (the authors estimate ≈ 70% with boards closer to final size) (Uí Chúláin et al., 2023).
  • Built example: Urban Machine's Oakland building at 1070 40th St used reclaimed-wood DLT made by All Bay Lumber, completed Oct 15, 2023 (Mass Timber Report). No tonnage was published.
  • Our role: supply graded, de-nailed lamstock plus per-piece data to a manufacturer. We do not build a panel plant T.

Business idea and competitive advantage

Q1If the wood is valuable, why hasn't an existing reclaimed-lumber business already turned it into benches?

EngineerShort pieces need a different process from volume lumber. Urbanjacks finger-joins and glues short sections into boards up to 20 ft. We keep pieces whole and unglued, bolted onto a frame with supports every 2 ft, so a slat can be unbolted and swapped.

BusinessProcessors sell lumber by volume to makers, museums, theatres and film studios. Installed, maintained public seating needs facilities sales and installation crews, a small, service-heavy niche. We don't claim they overlooked it, only that it doesn't fit a lumber model.

Evidence: Urbanjacks: fasteners removed, finger-joined, glued, up to 20 ft; sells at a 10–15% premium (Choi, 2025). Burnaby budgets $200 per bench per year for maintenance (Burnaby Now).

Q2One reuse outlet rejects boards under six feet. How does that prove there is no market for them?

EngineerA length rule reflects that outlet's handling, not the wood's strength. A full-size 2 in slat on 2-ft supports is stressed about 5.2 MPa, roughly 12× under salvaged clear-wood strength C, so 4–6 ft is usable for seating. Old wood keeps its strength: 75% of 55-year-old Douglas-fir columns still graded No. 2 or higher in USDA lab tests (Falk & Green, 1999).

BusinessIt doesn't prove there is no market, and we no longer claim that. Short pieces have uses: flooring from 3 ft and random-length paneling. Our narrower claim: local buyers are not absorbing most of the lumber today.

Evidence: ReStore: "Minimum of 6' long with no nails or paint" (MV directory). "Lumber shorter than 4' can only be recycled" (CoV, Grandview-Woodland). Flooring and paneling uses (Teshnizi, 2015). Riley Park reused 22% of lumber and sent 74% to waste-to-energy (CoV, Riley Park); Dunbar reused 25% (CoV, Dunbar).

Q3What stops an established bench manufacturer from copying your design and adding a provenance tag?

EngineerNothing stops the design; bolted benches are simple. What's hard to copy is process data: De-Nail Lab logs of minutes per board, reject rates and yield by house age. That data is what makes reclaimed slats predictable enough to sell.

BusinessFour things take time to build: contractor relationships through pre-demolition surveys, City-ready documentation, a per-piece source record, and maintenance contracts. If a manufacturer copies us, our best outcome is selling them graded slats.

Evidence: Deposit refunds require receipts for every material (CoV); the $15,310 deposit is refunded on a sliding scale (CoV).

Q4Are you running a demolition recovery service or a furniture business? Which one must succeed first?

EngineerBenches can run on any clean reclaimed 2×4 stock, so the product doesn't depend on our own recovery. Sort & Supply depends on de-nailing speed, which the De-Nail Lab measures first.

BusinessBenches must succeed first. Wood is about $29 of a $3,500 bench C; even at 3× that price it is under 3%. Sort & Supply alone breaks even only above about $549 per saleable tonne C. It starts as a light add-on once bench orders exist.

Evidence: Dunbar: $6,000 lumber sales ÷ 5.3 t = $1,132/t (Metro Vancouver). Grandview-Woodland: 13,005 kg lumber, 55% reused, 160 h de-nailing (CoV, Grandview-Woodland).

Q5Why manufacture benches instead of supplying short reclaimed boards to existing furniture makers?

EngineerA bench uses short boards at their best: whole, in compression and short-span bending on a 2-ft grid. Boards sold loose need a buyer whose design fits our random lengths.

BusinessValue per kilogram. De-nailed salvaged 2×4 sold for about $0.25–0.30 per foot; 26 kg of wood is worth about $29 C but supports a $3,500 installed product T. Selling milled slats to makers is our fallback (Q26).

Evidence: Salvaged prices (Teshnizi, 2015); Dunbar value (Metro Vancouver); Burnaby's $5,000 installed bench (Burnaby Now).

Design and operational feasibility

Q17You target four-to-six-foot boards, but your example uses six-foot slats. How will a four-foot board become part of a six-foot bench?

EngineerThe frame has cross-supports every 2 ft. Each slat row is one 6-ft piece or a 4-ft + 2-ft pair, with joints staggered between rows. Bending stress at a 2-ft gap is about 5.2 MPa versus 15.7 MPa unsupported over 6 ft C. Deck rules allow 24 in spacing for 2-in-thick wood and ask for three supports per board. A 4-ft slat on 0/2/4 ft supports meets both. The engineer's load test confirms with real, knotty boards. Stiffness matters too: with E = 1.95 × 10⁶ psi (USDA Wood Handbook), a 1,500 N point load deflects a full-size slat about 0.5 mm on a 2-ft gap, versus about 13 mm across 6 ft unsupported (δ = PL³ ÷ 48EI) C. Boxed-heart pieces with heart checks are tested or rejected, since checks cut beam strength about 15% (Falk & Green, 1999).

BusinessThis widens usable supply to the whole 4–6 ft band and keeps a 4-ft Model S (48 in benches are a standard commercial size) as a second product. More supports may raise frame cost, so the fabricator quote must use the 2-ft grid.

Evidence: (IRC Table R507.7); (AWC DCA 6); (USDA Wood Handbook); (Teshnizi, 2015); (Global Industrial).

Q18Is your first product backed or backless? Does it use five full-size slats or eight modern-size slats?

EngineerModel L: backless, five full-size slats (true 2 in × 4 in). 5 × 0.0508 × 0.1016 × 1.829 m = 0.0472 m³ × 545 kg/m³ ≈ 26 kg C. A backed version adds about 3 slats (≈41 kg) and backrest load tests.

BusinessBackless is cheaper and faster to approve. The backed model follows only if a buyer's accessibility spec requires a backrest; CSA/ASC B651:23 is Canada's accessible-design standard and facilities may reference it.

Evidence: (Teshnizi, 2015); (USDA Wood Handbook); (CSA/ASC B651:23).

Q19How will you produce consistent benches from wood with different dimensions, moisture levels, and defects?

EngineerDe-Nail Lab grading: accept only clean wood; reject paint (pre-1960 homes probably contain lead paint), treatment, glue, rot, splits and knots at bolt holes. Measure moisture with a meter and accept dry stock (S-Dry means 19% or less), because moisture change causes shrinkage and swelling. Coast Douglas-fir shrinks 7.6% tangentially and 4.8% radially from green to oven-dry (USDA Wood Handbook); below the ≈28% fibre saturation point (Canadian Wood Council), a 7-point moisture swing moves a 4-in slat about 2 mm across its width C. So slats are spaced with a 1/8 in gap, as deck guides specify after drying (AWC DCA 6), and bolt holes are slotted T. Mill every slat to one cross-section. Grade the way the USDA Forest Products Laboratory did: treat nail and bolt holes as an equivalent knot size, and expect recycled lumber to grade about one grade lower than new (68% of their recycled 2×4s graded Standard) (Falk & Green, 1999).

BusinessPlan for about half to be rejected: only 22–55% of lumber was reused in local deconstructions, and 218 of 483 salvaged 2×4s in Portland were too short to grade. Rejects are priced into the 60% yield.

Evidence: (MV clean-wood card); (Health Canada); (Canadian Wood Council); (CoV, Riley Park); (CoV, Grandview-Woodland); (Arbelaez et al., 2019).

Q20What happens if a contractor needs the wood removed tomorrow, but you have no storage space or bench orders?

EngineerThe De-Nail Lab holds a capped buffer of about one pallet (roughly 10 benches' worth), stored dry and off the ground to protect moisture content; wood exposed to moisture gains it back and moves (Canadian Wood Council).

BusinessThen we decline and the contractor demolishes as planned. We work buyer-first and survey early: a Hazardous Building Materials report is required before demolition, which creates lead time. One house's 4–6 ft fraction can supply about 19 benches at a 10% share C.

Evidence: (CoV); (Canadian Wood Council); Teshnizi framing estimate (Teshnizi, 2015).

Q21How do you know the $300 allowance can cover engineering, warranty reserves, and overhead at a production volume of one?

EngineerA prototype needs its own design review and load test, a one-time cost. Later units reuse the stamped design. The USDA lab tested salvaged members to ASTM D198 static methods (Falk & Green, 1999); our engineer sets the equivalent proof test for slats and the full bench.

BusinessIt can't, and we don't claim it does. The $300 is a batch figure ($150 engineering share over 10 units + $150 warranty reserve). Prototype engineering is a startup cost paid from the pilot budget. We will get an engineer's quote and spread it over the first 10 units.

Evidence: Model on this page; quote pending.

Extra: prototyping and material engineering

Q29What exactly will your prototype test, and in what order?

EngineerA five-step test plan:

  1. Grade every slat the USDA way, counting nail and bolt holes as an equivalent knot size (Falk & Green, 1999).
  2. Check moisture: accept 19% or less (the S-Dry limit) (Canadian Wood Council).
  3. Proof-load sample slats on a 2-ft span. The engineer sets the load and method, using ASTM D198 static bending as the reference method (Falk & Green, 1999).
  4. Test the assembled bench for static seat load and stability, using the test types in BIFMA X5.4 (static, dynamic, durability, stability; users up to 275 lb) as a checklist (BIFMA X5.4 via Micom).
  5. Put it outside for one season at a pilot site, with monthly checks of moisture, checks and bolt corrosion T.

BusinessEvery step also produces a cost number: grading reject rate, minutes per slat and failed parts. Those feed the calculator directly.

Evidence: (Falk & Green, 1999); (Canadian Wood Council); (BIFMA X5.4 via Micom).

Q30How strong is a salvaged slat, really, compared with new lumber?

EngineerClear Coast Douglas-fir has a bending strength of 12,400 psi (≈ 85 MPa) (USDA Wood Handbook via Virginia Tech). Salvaged lumber tested about 25% lower in bending (Teshnizi, 2015). Recycled dimension lumber graded on average "one grade lower" than new, and 68% of recycled 2×4s graded Standard (Falk & Green, 1999). Our 2-ft grid keeps peak stress at about 5.2 MPa under 1,500 N C, which is why short, lower-grade pieces still work for seating, but never for building structure.

BusinessLower grade is why short salvaged 2×4s are cheap. Seating is a use where that grade is enough, which is our value gap.

Evidence: (USDA Wood Handbook via Virginia Tech); (Teshnizi, 2015); (Falk & Green, 1999); spacing rule (IRC Table R507.7).

Q31How do you keep hazardous wood out of a public bench?

EngineerScreening happens twice. First, before demolition, from the City-required Hazardous Building Materials report. Second, at the De-Nail Lab. Reject anything painted (homes built before 1960 probably contain lead paint (Health Canada)), and decks or fences likely treated with CCA before 2004 (Health Canada). Never sand or burn unknown coatings.

BusinessRejects stay with the contractor and are recorded on the receipt, so we never pay to dispose of, or claim credit for, wood we didn't accept.

Evidence: (Health Canada); (Health Canada).

Q32Will short pieces give enough consistent slats to build in batches?

EngineerPiece length is the unknown. In Portland, 267 of 483 salvaged 2×4s were shorter than 2.3 m and 218 were too short to grade (Arbelaez et al., 2019). Our 2-ft grid accepts 2, 4 and 6 ft cuts, so most pieces of 4 ft or more yield at least one slat. The piece-length log on one house measures this.

BusinessBatch size follows supply: about 19 benches per house at a 10% share of 4–6 ft lumber C. Model S (4 ft) absorbs pieces that are too short for Model L.

Evidence: (Arbelaez et al., 2019); framing per house (Teshnizi, 2015).

Price is the weak point: at Richmond-level prices the base case holds (+$1,520), but at Surrey's low end the margin is almost zero. Two numbers decide the venture, the 4–6 ft share per house and the price a real buyer pays, so the pilot measures those first.
